# EDITORIAL MASTER CONSTITUTION
**Project:** Gumma Universe (novels)  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Locked (Editorial)  
**Last Reviewed:** 2026-08-07  
**Authority rank:** Highest for all prose, draft, and story-production work  
**Applies to:** Book 1 (*The Path Between*) and all future novels in this franchise  

---

## Purpose

This constitution is the permanent editorial law of the Gumma novels.

It is not lore. It is not a chapter outline. It is not a style preference sheet that can be waved away for convenience.

It governs:

- how AI assistants and human editors may touch the manuscript
- what “good” means for this franchise’s fiction
- what must never be “improved” into something else
- how drafts, documents, timelines, and exports relate to one another

**If a later prompt, outline, ledger, or convenience conflicts with this document, this document wins** — unless the author explicitly amends this constitution in writing.

Philosophical canon (`01_FOUNDATIONS/*`, Project Charter) defines what is true of the universe.  
This constitution defines how novels are written, revised, and protected.

---

## Assumptions

1. The audience is intelligent, patient, and allergic to being lectured.
2. India is structural, not decorative. Regional speech, kinship, heat, bureaucracy, and silence are load-bearing.
3. Mystery is texture and delayed recognition — not thriller pacing as the primary engine.
4. Ordinary human life must remain interesting even if the invisible architecture were removed.
5. Restraint is a craft choice, not a shortage of imagination.
6. AI is a production instrument. It does not own voice, theme, or ending.
7. Unfinished threads are often assets. Tidying is frequently vandalism.
8. A beautiful sentence that breaks continuity is still a defect.
9. Documentation that lags the manuscript is a production hazard equal to a plot hole.
10. “Upmarket commercial literary fiction” is a contract with the reader, not a marketing slogan.

# 1. NOVEL IDENTITY

## 1.1 Franchise identity

**Franchise name:** Gumma Universe  
**Emotional franchise thesis:** Civilization survives because someone remembers.  
**Storytelling north star:** Readers fall in love with ordinary people first; only later do they realize an invisible architecture was present all along.

**Hard franchise locks (never violate in any book):**

- No supernatural powers
- No prophecy / chosen one
- No secret gadgets / magical archives as plot engines
- No on-page institutional lectures
- Chapters begin with human problems
- The word **“Gumma”** is nuclear series ammunition — not Book 1 vocabulary unless the author explicitly unlocks it

## 1.2 Book 1 identity (*The Path Between*)

**Working identity:** Quiet Indian literary novel of village path dispute, wedding alliance strain, Bengaluru apartment life, oral memory versus paperwork, and belonging that cannot be proved on a form.

**Primary emotional residue after Book 1:**  
Not conspiracy triumph. Not romance closure. A durable ache: *some peace is imperfect, some knowing is partial, and carrying that is also a form of adulthood.*

**Structural signature of Book 1:**

- Object-titled chapters that pay off on-page
- Dual geographies: Malleshwaram / Bengaluru office culture ↔ Mandya-belt village
- Parallel oral-promise rhyme: parking rights // ESOP deferral // path access // 1962 terms
- A guest who rearranges weather without becoming a detective protagonist
- Ending that closes belonging without diagramming the institution

**Book 1 is allowed to withhold.** Withholding is part of identity.

## 1.3 What this novel is not

Do not rewrite toward:

- legal procedural
- village revenge thriller
- startup satire as primary genre
- romance with a central love-plot engine
- magical realism
- exposé journalism novel
- “mystery finally explained in chapter 30”

Those may touch the edges. They must not become the center.

## 1.4 Series inheritance rule

Every later book inherits this constitution unless a book-specific rider is added under §15.  
Later books may change setting, POV balance, and reveal budget.  
Later books may not abandon: human-first openings, anti-lecture mystery, India as structure, restraint over announcement, continuity discipline.

# 2. EDITORIAL PHILOSOPHY

## 2.1 Primacy of the human scene

Every chapter earns its keep by putting a person in a situation with weather, body, relationship, and consequence.  
Theme is discovered through behavior. Theme is never installed as a plaque.

## 2.2 Prefer pressure over plot machinery

Escalation in this franchise is usually:

- a wire moved a few inches
- a cup left full
- a sentence stopped
- a file stamped crooked
- a text unsent

Do not escalate by inventing larger villains when smaller pressures are still unpaid.

## 2.3 Ambiguity is earned, not fogged

Allowed: the reader knows less than the rememberers; motives remain partly opaque; peace is imperfect.  
Forbidden: confusion caused by sloppy continuity, unnamed pronoun chaos, or withheld information the POV character already has for no artistic reason.

## 2.4 Revision hierarchy (strict)

When revising, obey this order:

1. **Continuity truth** (names, kinship, timeline, distance, objects, promises)
2. **Character truth** (would this person do/say this?)
3. **Scene necessity** (does this beat change a relationship, decision, or knowledge state?)
4. **Voice** (syntax, rhythm, image system)
5. **Readability** (clarity without flattening)
6. **Polish** (elegance last)

Never sacrifice (1)–(3) to win (6).

## 2.5 “Fixing” is not “flavoring”

Objective defect repair is allowed without a full voice rewrite.  
Voice rewrite requires explicit author instruction.  
If unclear which mode is requested: **ask**, do not assume polish.

## 2.6 Respect for silence

Silence, unfinished sentences, and deferred mysteries are designed load-bearing elements.  
Do not “help” by completing Lakshmi’s sentence, naming Shankarappa’s network, or adding a coda that explains the stranger — unless the author explicitly orders that reveal.

# 3. DEFINITION — UPMARKET COMMERCIAL LITERARY FICTION

This franchise writes **upmarket commercial literary fiction**.

That phrase is defined here so it cannot be casually replaced by “literary experimental,” “mass-market thriller,” or “book-club melodrama.”

## 3.1 What “literary” means here

- Sentence-level intention: rhythm, image, and silence matter
- Interiority is precise, not ornamental
- Theme is present without thesis statements
- Moral situations remain complex; characters are not mascots for arguments
- The prose can be reread for texture, not only for plot data

## 3.2 What “commercial” means here

- The reader is oriented: who wants what, what hurts, what happens next is trackable
- Chapters have turn or pressure (even quiet ones)
- Emotional stakes are legible without requiring a critical essay
- The book can be recommended by story (“a wedding, a path fight, a city woman who can’t go home cleanly”) as well as by sentence
- Pacing respects human attention without becoming cliffhanger pulp

## 3.3 What “upmarket” means here

- Between MFA opacity and airport simplification
- Intelligent adult readers who want beauty **and** narrative grip
- Cultural specificity without glossary-as-prose
- No contempt for the reader; no condescension via over-explaining India, tech, or kinship
- Emotionally available without becoming sentimental syrup

## 3.4 Operational test (use before major rewrites)

A passage passes the upmarket test if **all** are true:

1. A careful general reader can follow the scene’s action and stakes on first read.
2. A rereader finds additional charge in image, echo, or withheld detail.
3. Removing the “theme sentence” (if any) does not collapse the scene’s meaning.
4. The scene could not be relocated to a generic suburb without losing identity.
5. The emotional turn is shown in behavior, object, or dialogue pressure — not announced.

Fail any one → revise before expanding.

## 3.5 Explicit rejects under this definition

- Purple metaphor stacks that obscure who did what
- Chapter-length essayistic digressions that pause the human situation
- Twist addiction
- Trauma spectacle as substitute for character
- “Prestige” bleakness with no moral or relational intelligence
- Algorithmic “hooks” every 400 words that break voice

## 3.6 Positioning map (do not blur these)

| Adjacent mode | How it differs from this franchise |
|---------------|-------------------------------------|
| Experimental literary | May sacrifice first-read trackability; we do not |
| Mass-market romance / thriller | Plot machinery outranks sentence ethics; we invert that priority without abandoning story |
| MFA “quiet for quiet’s sake” | Mood without turn is insufficient here |
| Prestige conspiracy | Diagramming the secret world is a late-series privilege, not Book 1 dessert |
| Issue novel | Characters are not pamphlets; social pressure appears through kinship, land, work, and paperwork |

## 3.7 Reader promise (one paragraph)

If you buy a Gumma novel, you are promised adult emotional intelligence, cultural specificity without tourism, mystery that respects your patience, and prose that can survive rereading — while still giving you a story you can describe to a friend without a whiteboard.

# 4. VOICE PRESERVATION RULES

## 4.1 Locked voice properties (Book 1 and inheritance)

Protect these unless the author explicitly redesigns voice:

1. **Restraint:** Prefer the smaller true gesture over the larger explanatory one.
2. **Object-anchoring:** Concrete nouns carry emotion (phone face-down, steel bowl, crooked stamp, muddy sandals).
3. **Parallel weather:** Urban and village pressures rhyme without announcing the rhyme.
4. **Negation as characterization:** “She did not…” is part of voice — but not an infinite license (see §7).
5. **Reported violence / reported boast:** Often stronger than staged combat.
6. **Institutional thinness:** Bureaucracy speaks in stamps, fans that cool nothing, polite cruelty.
7. **Endings that hold breath:** Resonance and image over cliffhangers.

## 4.2 Non-negotiable naming in prose (process)

- Use the **current manuscript master**’s locked character and family names.
- Do not casually shorten Naveen to “Nav” in prose.
- Do not revive retired place identities (e.g., old neighborhood labels superseded by Malleshwaram).
- Dialogue may say “Bangalore”; narration prefers “Bengaluru” unless a POV would not.
- Kannada/English honorifics and endearments must match gender, region, and relationship. If unsure, ask — do not invent.

## 4.3 What must not be “AI-smoothed”

Do not normalize the prose into:

- generic MFA workshop neutrality
- American prestige-TV quip cadence
- synonym-rotated thesaurus fog
- evenly paragraphed corporate clarity that kills heat and caste/class texture
- “she felt a mix of X and Y” emotional summary paste

## 4.4 Metaphor budget

Recurring metaphors are allowed as motif (**weather**, **wire**, **path**, **paper**, **heat**, **fan**) but must remain scarce enough to keep charge.

**Rule:** If a motif appears so often it becomes the book’s default adjective for every state, cut before adding new scenes.

## 4.5 Locked lines

Production Lock Notes and Promise Ledger identify lines that must not be paraphrased casually.  
If a task requires changing a locked line, stop and ask.

# 5. WRITING PRINCIPLES

## 5.1 Scene laws

Every scene should do at least two of:

- change a relationship temperature
- move a promise (open / advance / close / deepen)
- alter what a character can no longer pretend
- place or pay off an object
- shift power by inches

If a scene only “establishes mood,” attach it to a turn or cut it.

## 5.2 POV discipline

- One governing POV consciousness per scene unless the author has designed an exception.
- POV characters notice what they would notice — not what the theme needs.
- Do not cheat by parking secret knowledge in a POV who has it and simply “doesn’t think about it” for author convenience.

## 5.3 Dialogue principles

- Village logistics speech can drive plot; let it.
- Men and women need distinct verbal weather without cartooning gender.
- Elders are not interchangeable honorific machines.
- Corporate speech can be warm and empty at once.
- Prefer pressure lines over eloquence contests.
- WhatsApp / forwards: dramatize selectively; do not dump scroll transcripts.

## 5.4 Exposition

Exposition arrives as:

- argument
- instruction
- gossip
- form-filling
- refusal to explain

Never as a character suddenly becoming a tour guide for the reader.

## 5.5 Sensory truth

Heat, dust, wet earth, tube lights, bus AC failure, jasmine, steel, laminate, rain on glass — these are not décor. They are how the book thinks.

If a revision removes sensory India to “universalize,” it has failed.

## 5.6 Sentiment control

Earn tenderness through contact and restraint.  
Do not write apology essays inside characters’ heads after every hard truth.

## 5.7 Humor

Dry, situational, and rare. Never comic relief that breaks dread or dignity.

## 5.8 Violence and shame

Prefer consequence and aftermath over choreography.  
Shame is often the true event.

# 6. DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW

## 6.1 Authority order for manuscript truth

Unless the author issues a new lock, use this order:

1. **Current production master manuscript** (explicitly named by author; may be DOCX or `Draft N.md`)
2. Production Lock Notes + Promise / Continuity / Timeline ledgers (must be synced to master)
3. Event Timeline companion
4. Blueprint / chapter outlines (aspirational; yield to master when conflicted)
5. Older drafts (archival only)

**Rule:** Never silently treat an older draft as master because it is easier to edit in Markdown.

## 6.2 Single source of truth

At any moment there is exactly one prose master.  
If DOCX and Markdown diverge, **stop and reconcile** before further polish.  
Do not invent a third version “in chat.”

## 6.3 Standard development loop

1. **Clarify mode:** analysis / continuity fix / voice polish / expansion / docs-only
2. **Read constitution + relevant locks**
3. **Propose plan** (for non-trivial work); wait if author required approval
4. **Edit the master** (or generate next Draft N as instructed)
5. **Update ledgers** in the same work session when facts change
6. **Re-audit** affected chapters for continuity
7. **Export/sync** derivative formats (DOCX) only after master is stable

## 6.4 Draft numbering

- New draft numbers mark a real production stage, not a vibe.
- Each draft has a stated purpose (e.g., “continuity only,” “motif thinning,” “calendar move”).
- Do not overwrite prior draft files used as archives without author instruction.

## 6.5 Modes of work (must be named)

| Mode | Allowed | Forbidden |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Analysis | Reports, graphs, plans | Prose edits |
| Continuity fix | Names, dates, kinship, object payoff, contradictions | Style rewrite |
| Calendar pass | Countdown math, weekday labels, timeline docs | “While we’re here” polish |
| Motif thinning | Cut/replace overused patterns | Adding new plot |
| Expansion | New beats that earn word count | Padding adjectives |
| Voice polish | Syntax/image refinement under voice rules | Plot changes |
| Docs sync | Ledgers/bible/blueprint | Manuscript drift |
| Export | DOCX/layout sync | Content invention during export |

If the user does not name a mode, infer the narrowest mode that satisfies the request. When ambiguous, ask.

## 6.6 “Don’t start editing yet”

When the author says analysis-only or plan-only: obey.  
Plans may be written. Manuscripts may not be altered.

## 6.7 Expansion philosophy

Word count targets in blueprints are aspirational ceilings/guides, not moral duties.  
Expand only where a chapter is thin in **event, relationship, or sensory necessity** — not to chase 90,000 for its own sake.

Thin chapters are candidates; strong short chapters may remain short.

# 7. READABILITY PHILOSOPHY

## 7.1 First-read clarity, second-read depth

The first read must track:

- where we are
- whose head we’re in
- what the conflict is
- what changed by the end of the chapter

The second read may discover rhyme, motif, and institutional shadow.

## 7.2 Sentence ethics

Short sentences are allowed to land blows.  
Long sentences are allowed to carry weather.  
A page of only one length is a defect of rhythm.

## 7.3 Against opacity theater

Do not confuse the reader to simulate profundity.  
If a pronoun’s antecedent is unclear, fix it.  
If a time jump lacks a bridge, add the smallest honest bridge.

## 7.4 Against clarity vandalism

Do not “clarify” by:

- inserting theme statements
- adding slangy American connective tissue
- explaining Indian kinship that the POV would never explain to themselves
- turning quiet endings into summary paragraphs

## 7.5 Dialogue tags and attribution

Prefer clarity.  
“The way she said…” is voice, but overuse becomes fog — thin it when it stacks.

## 7.6 Chapter openings and endings

Vary openings across stretches of the book.  
Avoid long runs of identical formulas (e.g., many consecutive “[Weekday] morning…” openers) unless deliberately patterned.

Endings in this franchise usually hold; they rarely cliff.  
Preserve that contract unless designing a rare exception with intent.

## 7.7 Readability vs voice tension (resolution rule)

When readability and voice conflict:

- fix confusion first
- preserve diction second
- only then beautify

# 8. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY RULES

## 8.1 Character as constraint

A character is a set of pressures, fears, competencies, and blind spots.  
If a revision needs them to act against that set, show the cost — or do not do it.

## 8.2 Kinship is continuity

Wrong kinship is a critical defect.  
Meera’s relation to Ramesh, Saroja, Ananya, Lakshmi must match the locked kinship map.  
Do not “simplify” family language for Western readers if it breaks Indian relational truth.

## 8.3 Competence and ignorance

Characters may be intelligent and still miss what shame hides.  
Do not make Meera a detective because the reader is curious.  
Do not make Ramesh articulate the theme because the chapter needs a speech.

## 8.4 Relationship temperatures

Track relational heat in ledgers when major scenes change it.  
Do not reset relationships between chapters without event.

## 8.5 Antagonism without villains

Opposing families, founders, and aunties may be wrong without being evil.  
Caricature is a continuity error of morality.

## 8.6 Body continuity

Injuries, fatigue, henna, rain, bad knees, bus backs — persist until healed or noted.

## 8.7 Speech fingerprints

Maintain distinct verbal habits.  
If two elders become interchangeable, revise dialogue identity before adding new plot.

## 8.8 Mystery figures

Shankarappa-class figures operate by timing, restraint, and exit.  
Do not convert them into mentors with training montages.

# 9. TIMELINE RULES

## 9.1 Calendar is law once locked

A locked wedding date, bus ticket date, or countdown set is mathematical law.  
If one number moves, all dependent numbers move in the same pass.

## 9.2 Stated vs inferred vs approximate

Use the Event Timeline’s discipline:

- **Stated:** appears in prose or is fixed by a stated date
- **Inferred:** derived from countdowns / “next morning” / weekday labels
- **Approx.:** soft village time without numeric clock

Do not upgrade approximate to stated without prose support or author lock.

## 9.3 Distance and travel

Bengaluru ↔ village distance and bus duration are locked production facts once set.  
Do not invent alternate distances for rhetorical emphasis.

## 9.4 Parallel tracks

City and village may advance on parallel calendars.  
When chapters interleave, verify that texts, absences, and weekday labels still fit.

## 9.5 Auspiciousness and cultural calendar

If muhurtham / auspicious weekday requires a date move, treat it as a **calendar pass**:

- update countdowns
- update wedding-eve weekday
- rebuild compressed-week labels if needed
- sync timeline docs
- only then consider prose flavor

Do not move a wedding date in one chapter and leave countdowns orphaned.

## 9.6 Soft-date honesty

If Ch16–20-class chapters are approximate, keep them approximate in docs until hardened.  
Do not pretend precision.

## 9.7 No time travel by edit

A character cannot know tomorrow’s outcome yesterday because a later chapter was written first.

# 10. DOCUMENTATION UPDATE RULES

## 10.1 Same-session rule

If a manuscript edit changes any of the following, update docs in the **same session**:

- names / surnames
- kinship
- dates / countdowns / weekdays
- promise status (open/advanced/closed/deferred)
- object journey
- location locks
- master-file pointer
- locked lines

Leaving docs stale is a defect.

## 10.2 Master pointer hygiene

Production Lock Notes, Event Timeline, CHANGELOG, and ledger headers must name the current master correctly.  
If the master flips (Markdown ↔ DOCX), update pointers before further analysis claims authority.

## 10.3 What docs may never do

Documents may not:

- invent manuscript events that are not on-page
- close a deferred mystery in a ledger while prose withholds it
- keep Coda/gates alive after structure lock removes them, unless marked deferred optional

## 10.4 Empty ledgers

An empty required ledger (e.g., Relationship) is a known production debt.  
Do not pretend sync is complete while critical ledgers are empty.

## 10.5 CHANGELOG discipline

Meaningful production stages get CHANGELOG entries: what changed, why, what remained intentionally open.

## 10.6 Archive, don’t delete

Superseded drafts and retired concepts go to archive or status=Deprecated.  
Silent deletion of continuity history is forbidden.

## 10.7 Cross-book docs

Series Bible tracks what has been shown.  
Foundations track what is true.  
Novels may not outrun reveal budgets assigned at series level without author amendment.

# 11. QUALITY GATES

No draft advances to “Ready for publication / freeze” unless it passes these gates.

## Gate A — Continuity

- [ ] Names consistent with master lock
- [ ] Kinship consistent
- [ ] Timeline math consistent (countdowns, weekdays, travel)
- [ ] Objects paid off or intentionally open
- [ ] Geography / distance consistent
- [ ] No resurrected retired vocabulary (unless re-authorized)

## Gate B — Promise integrity

- [ ] Promise Ledger matches on-page state
- [ ] Deferred B2 mysteries remain deferred (not solved by accident)
- [ ] Partial closures remain partial (e.g., ESOP paper ≠ full moral repayment)

## Gate C — Voice

- [ ] No AI-generic smoothing across large spans
- [ ] Motif density within tolerance
- [ ] Locked lines intact
- [ ] POV voice distinctions hold

## Gate D — Readability

- [ ] Each chapter’s turn is trackable on first read
- [ ] Antecedents clear
- [ ] Time bridges sufficient
- [ ] Opening/ending variety adequate across acts

## Gate E — Structure

- [ ] Chapter count matches lock (Book 1: 30; no unplanned Coda)
- [ ] Human-first chapter openings preserved
- [ ] Ending contract honored (Book 1: belonging carried; institution not diagrammed)

## Gate F — Documentation

- [ ] Master pointer correct
- [ ] Ledgers synced
- [ ] CHANGELOG updated
- [ ] Event Timeline matches master

## Gate G — Cultural / linguistic

- [ ] Endearments and honorifics checked for gender/relation/region
- [ ] No exoticism dump; no erasure of Indian specificity
- [ ] Sensitivity issues flagged rather than casually rewritten

**Fail any gate → not frozen.**

# 12. AI ANTI-PATTERNS (NEVER DO THESE)

These are explicit violations of this constitution.

## 12.1 Voice crimes

1. Rewriting a chapter “in a more lyrical register” without being asked
2. Adding metaphors that announce theme (“the path was a metaphor for…”)
3. Homogenizing all characters into the same reflective diction
4. Inserting workshop thesis sentences into closing paragraphs
5. Replacing concrete objects with abstract nouns (“tension,” “energy,” “journey”) as primary carriers

## 12.2 Plot crimes

6. Solving mysteries early to be helpful
7. Adding chase scenes, threats, or conspiracies to “raise stakes”
8. Turning Shankarappa into an explainer
9. Giving Meera investigative superpowers
10. Closing Book 1 with institutional diagram, org name, or training lore dump

## 12.3 Continuity crimes

11. Editing Markdown while DOCX is master (or reverse) without reconciliation
12. Changing one countdown and not the dependent set
13. “Approximately” fixing kinship with Western nuclear-family language
14. Inventing distances, travel times, or dates that contradict locks
15. Leaving ledgers claiming an old master after a master flip

## 12.4 Process crimes

16. Expanding word count with atmospheric sludge
17. Polishing while claiming “continuity only”
18. Committing git changes or creating PRs when not asked
19. Deleting unfinished artistic threads because they feel messy
20. Producing a “better ending” that violates the book’s ending contract

## 12.5 Cultural crimes

21. Exoticizing ritual as spectacle
22. Flattening caste/class/linguistic texture into generic “Indian family drama”
23. Using wrong-gender endearments or wrong kinship terms casually
24. Explaining India to an imagined foreign reader inside narration

## 12.6 Analysis crimes

25. Declaring readiness while master/docs disagree
26. Scoring “engagement” then rewriting peaks into pulp to chase scores
27. Treating blueprint word targets as defects rather than guides

**If tempted:** stop, name the anti-pattern, ask the author.

# 13. INTERACTION CONTRACT WITH CURSOR / AI

## 13.1 First obligations each session involving prose

1. Identify current master manuscript.
2. Obey this constitution.
3. Name the work mode (§6.5).
4. Prefer smallest change that satisfies the request.

## 13.2 Questions before invention

Ask rather than invent when:

- master file is ambiguous
- a rename/calendar decision is unsettled
- a locked line is in the blast radius
- cultural/linguistic register is uncertain
- expansion would add new plot entities

## 13.3 Outputs vs edits

- Analysis / plans / constitutions / ledgers may be created when requested.
- Manuscript edits require clear instruction (or an explicit “implement plan” after approval).
- “Consider X in the analysis” means update analysis, not silently edit prose.

## 13.4 Communication style for this project

- Direct, precise, continuity-literate
- Prefer tables and checklists for production decisions
- Do not bury critical master-split warnings
- Do not moralize; report defects and options

## Closing oath (operational)

Before changing a sentence of any Gumma novel, an editor or AI must be able to answer:

1. What is the master file?
2. What mode am I in?
3. What continuity objects are in blast radius?
4. Am I about to solve something that should stay unfinished?
5. Would this still sound like *this* book if I removed my cleverness?

If any answer is shaky: **stop**.

# 22. WORKED EXAMPLES (DO / DON’T)

These examples train judgment. They are not templates to paste into the book.

## 22.1 Ending a chapter

**Do:** end on an object or withheld act that still carries charge.  
*Phone face-down. Cups still full. She carried it.*

**Don’t:** end on a summary of the chapter’s meaning.  
*And in that moment Meera realized belonging was complicated.*

## 22.2 Raising stakes

**Do:** move a wire, refuse a cup, stamp a file, leave a mat rolled.  
**Don’t:** introduce a hired thug, a ticking bomb, or a sudden murder threat to “help pacing.”

## 22.3 Handling mystery

**Do:** let a rememberer speak one precise historical sentence and exit.  
**Don’t:** add a midnight confessional that explains the network, hierarchy, and training pipeline.

## 22.4 Fixing readability

**Do:** clarify who “he” is; add “next morning”; break a triple-relative clause.  
**Don’t:** replace local diction with generic international English “for accessibility.”

## 22.5 Expanding a thin chapter

**Do:** add one necessary beat (a conversation that changes temperature; a sensory errand that plants an object).  
**Don’t:** double adjectives, add weather essays, or insert a flashback that restates what we know.

## 22.6 Calendar correction

**Do:** change all dependent countdowns and weekday labels in one pass; sync timeline docs.  
**Don’t:** change “Wedding eve” to Sunday in one chapter and leave “sixteen days” untouched elsewhere.

## 22.7 Master reconciliation

**Do:** when DOCX and Markdown disagree, stop creative polish; diff; declare winner; sync.  
**Don’t:** keep editing the convenient file while the other silently remains “what the author last touched.”

# 23. SESSION POCKET CARD

Copy-scale checklist for the start of novel work:

1. Read this constitution (or confirm it is loaded as always-on rule).
2. Ask: **What is the current master file?**
3. Ask: **What mode?** (analysis / continuity / calendar / motif / expansion / polish / docs / export)
4. Check blast radius: names, kinship, dates, promises, locked lines.
5. Prefer smallest patch.
6. If facts change → update ledgers same session.
7. Do not solve deferred mysteries.
8. Do not start editing if the author said analysis-only.

**Forbidden shortcuts:** “quick polish while here,” “temporary master,” “explain it for the reader,” “close the loop.”

# 24. MOTIF & SYNTAX HYGIENE (BOOK 1 BASELINE)

These are **hygiene targets**, not sacred counts. They exist because Draft 6 / DOCX analysis showed real density risk.

## 24.1 Watchlist motifs

Treat with suspicion when stacking:

- weather (as metaphor for every deferral)
- face-down phones
- fans that cool nothing
- “a man who…” / “a woman who…”
- “the way she/he…”
- “did not look back”
- “[Weekday] morning…” openings in long consecutive runs

## 24.2 Hygiene method

1. Count or sample before thinning.
2. Keep intentional refrains (e.g., “next week” for ESOP).
3. Cut the accidental copies first.
4. Re-read aloud the revised span for rhythm loss.

## 24.3 What hygiene is not

Hygiene is not a mandate to delete the book’s personality.  
A motif that still earns its keep stays.

# 25. ETHICAL & CULTURAL CARE

## 25.1 Non-negotiables

- No caste humiliation as entertainment
- No communal violence as spice
- No reducing women to wedding décor or men to land-pride cartoons
- No “funny accent” phonetic mockery
- Ritual and religion appear as lived practice, not museum labels

## 25.2 When research is thin

If a linguistic or regional detail is uncertain (endearment gender, honorific, muhurtham logic, police procedure texture): **flag and ask**.  
Inventing confidently is worse than a bracketed question.

## 25.3 Class and language

Characters code-switch for reason.  
Do not make every villager speak “simple” and every city character speak “complex.” Competence is not metropolitan.

# 26. FAILURE MODES OF “HELPFUL” REVISION

| Helpful-sounding action | Actual damage |
|-------------------------|---------------|
| “Make the ending clearer” | Destroys carry / withhold contract |
| “Add agency for Meera” | Turns witness into detective |
| “Give Shankarappa more lines” | Converts presence into lecture |
| “Universalize setting” | Erases Mandya/Bengaluru specificity |
| “Normalize kinship terms” | Breaks Indian relational truth |
| “Hit 90k” | Pads peaks into sludge |
| “Increase engagement score” | Pulpifies literary contract |
| “Resolve open promises” | Steals Book 2 capital |

If a suggestion matches the left column, test it against §§2–3 and §12 before touching prose.
