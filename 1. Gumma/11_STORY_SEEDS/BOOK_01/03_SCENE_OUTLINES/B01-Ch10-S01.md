## 1. Scene Objective
Deliver the Act I climax: path confrontation escalates to physical violence. Prasad shoves Hegde worker into fence post; post snaps; blood on dirt. The line crosses. Reader's last comfortable assumption — that this stays verbal — dies.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Vigilant → rushing → frozen shock → dread.

## 3. Scene Conflict
Two proxy warriors meet on the narrow path; neither yields; Ramesh approaching but too far; words escalate to contact; the shove is harder than intended.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
The path is physically narrow (wire-moved section); neither man can pass without one yielding; neither was instructed to yield; Ramesh's call ("Leave it") arrives too late; Prasad is young and hotheaded; the fence post is at the exact wrong angle.

## VII. Dialogue Opportunities
- Confrontation: BRIEF, HOT — "This is our side" / "All sides are for walking." Two-three sentences maximum before physical.
- Ramesh: "Leave it!" — shouted from distance. One call. Too late.
- Post-violence: SILENCE. No one speaks for three seconds.
- Forbidden: extended argument; political speeches; path history in confrontation; Prasad delivering villain lines; narrator moralizing.
- The scene's power is in its BREVITY. Violence takes 3 seconds; everything before is buildup; everything after is silence.

## XI. Blocking Notes
- Violence = ONE shove. Not a fight. Not punches. Not wrestling. Hands flat on chest. One push. Post does the injury.
- Three seconds silence: hold this in prose. Let one sentence be white space.
- Ramesh arrives AFTER — he witnesses the aftermath forming, not the contact moment itself (he's rushing toward).
- Prasad's terror must be visible (his face = the scene's moral complexity).
- Older Gowda man: his pulling-back is instinctive damage control, not apology.
- Worker's injury: bruised shoulder + scraped face. Painful, visible, NOT life-threatening. NOT hospitalized.
- Fence post breaks at base — physical evidence that will persist.

## XVII. Drafting Traps
- Action-movie choreography (this is ONE shove, not a fight scene).
- Narrator moralizing about violence.
- Making Prasad evil/cartoon (he's terrified after).
- Extended argument before the shove (keep words to 2-3 sentences maximum).
- Making injury melodramatic (it's real but not critical).
- Explaining rural violence as social phenomenon.
- Institutional vocabulary or "if only" constructions.
- Ramesh arriving in time to prevent it (he cannot — that's the point).
- Multiple rounds of violence (one shove; one fall; done).
