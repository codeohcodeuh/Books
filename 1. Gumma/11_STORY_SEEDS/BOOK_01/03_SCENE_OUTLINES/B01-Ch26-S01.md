## 1. Scene Objective
P-PATH closes as usable. P-PAPER closes. The path dispute resolves through bodies, not paper. Ananya walks to the path on a wet Monday morning (chappal stuck in mud). She discovers Ramesh and Venki walking it from opposite directions — they PASS each other without words, without fight, without detour. Footprints overlap in soft mud. Meera arrives. Two women witness what they're seeing without naming it. **Shankarappa ABSENT from the path.** The resolution happens without him — proving: the institution doesn't DO peace; it enables conditions for people to make their own.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Cautious approach (why is she going to the path?) → held breath (they're going to pass each other) → relief (they did; no fight; no words; just bodies on shared ground) → quiet joy-grief (it cost so much to get here; and this is what it looks like — not victory, not ceremony; just mud and morning and overlapping prints).

## 3. Scene Conflict
Ananya fears the path (the fight happened here; her wedding was poisoned by it). Walking to it is a small act of courage. Then: finding it occupied by BOTH men simultaneously is the held breath. The conflict is resolved by what DOESN'T happen — no fight, no detour, no words. The resolution is: absence of conflict lived into. Bodies agreeing before egos.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
She can't make them greet each other (that would be forced). She can't celebrate (that would be premature). She can only watch and hold the fragile moment — and accept that THIS is what peace looks like. Not handshakes. Not documents. Just: two men walking in opposite directions on shared ground without stopping.
