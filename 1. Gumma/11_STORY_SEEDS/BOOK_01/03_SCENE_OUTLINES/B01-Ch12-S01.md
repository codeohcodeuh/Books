## 1. Scene Objective
Introduce Shankarappa through Meera's observing eyes at the wedding-eve gathering. She notices elders from BOTH sides deferring to a quiet, unremarkable man — without any explanation or dramatic framing. Plant P-GUEST. Show the wedding-eve emotional cocktail of forced festivity and buried anger.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Performing cheer → observant alertness → quietly unsettled (something she can't name).

## 3. Scene Conflict
Meera wants to enjoy the evening but can't stop noticing: the performed smiles; the elephant of path-violence; and specifically — a guest who matters to old people for no visible reason. The social cost of watching (staring = breach of etiquette) constrains her observation.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
Wedding-eve etiquette punishes curiosity — she must perform belonging while filing observations. Every time she notices something odd (reorienting elders, Lakshmi's pause), she must look away or risk being the city cousin who stares too long.
