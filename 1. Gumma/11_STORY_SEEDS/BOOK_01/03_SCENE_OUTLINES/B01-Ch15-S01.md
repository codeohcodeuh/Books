**Working title:** You'll Go Back  
**Status:** READY FOR PROSE  
**Version:** 1.0

## 1. Scene Objective
Show Meera's mediation attempt failing — not because she's wrong, but because she lacks standing. Through Ananya's watching eyes, demonstrate that intelligence cannot substitute for legitimacy. The killing line ("You live in Bangalore. You'll go back.") names Meera's transience — and both sides agree on it, which is devastating.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Anxious hope (maybe Meera can do this) → cringe (she can't) → regret (for not helping; for watching someone try and fail).

## 3. Scene Conflict
Meera organized this gathering and believes reasonable conversation among the younger generation can "clear the air." She opens well. But the Hegde cousin names her insufficiency — you'll leave; you're not from here — and the Gowda-side youth nods in agreement. Both sides reject her authority for the same reason. Meera has no answer because it's true.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
Meera's transience (she WILL go back to Bangalore; she doesn't own land here; she hasn't been present for years of this dispute). Her intelligence is irrelevant because standing is what's needed, and standing requires presence over time — which she hasn't paid. Ananya's obstacle: she watches and doesn't intervene (guilty but also unable — what would she say?).

## IV. Scene Purpose

**Why this scene exists:** Establish that civilian intelligence cannot solve this dispute; set up the contrast with Shankarappa's different authority (Ch16); humiliate Meera in a way that primes the reader; show that BOTH sides agree Meera lacks standing.

**Removal test:** Meera never attempts mediation; the legitimacy question is never raised; Ch16's different authority has no contrast point; reader never sees why "someone smart" isn't enough.

**Promise work:** P-PATH advanced through failure — dispute proven unsolvable by civilian intervention.

**Relationship work:** Meera–cousins: 3 → 2 (she overstepped; they named her outsider). Ananya witnessing: bond with Meera deepens through shared vulnerability.

**Emotional work:** Hope → cringe → regret. Specifically: the pain of watching someone you like try and fail publicly.

**Institutional / RP work:** Zero. The argument FOR institutional function is implicit: if a smart civilian can't mediate, what can? The question is planted without any answer given.
