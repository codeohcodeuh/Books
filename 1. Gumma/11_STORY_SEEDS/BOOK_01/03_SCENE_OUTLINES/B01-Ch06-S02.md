## 1. Scene Objective
Deliver R1 — the novel's first RP beat — as body-memory triggered by stored blanket smell. Child Meera (~10, 2003) remembers a quiet man whose presence ended a shouting match. Sensory only. No name. No explanation. Sleep arrives carrying the ghost.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Defensive-tired → sensory surrender → fragile belonging.

## 3. Scene Conflict
Meera wants sleep but the room won't release her immediately; the blanket smell unlocks a memory she has no framework to interpret; belonging arrives involuntarily.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
The memory is incomplete — a 10-year-old's fragments; she cannot turn it into narrative; it comes and goes; sleep folds around it before she can process; adult Meera has no use for this memory and no conscious interest in it.

Meera: corridor → room entrance → bag down → bed/mat → horizontal → still. No movement after lying down. The memory is purely auditory/olfactory — no physical action during recall.

## VII. Dialogue Opportunities
- ZERO dialogue in present time.
- Memory: NO dialogue reproduced. Shouts = described as sound (volume, anger, male voices) not words. Quiet man: described as presence, not speech. Reader infers one sentence or sound but does not hear it.
- Forbidden: reproducing 2003 dialogue; giving the quiet man identifiable words; anyone speaking to present-Meera.

## IX. Internal Thought Opportunities
- Brief: adult Meera trying to hold the memory — "once, someone made it quiet."
- Brief: cannot name him; cannot see his face; only sound and smell.
- Forbidden: analyzing the memory; naming the man; connecting to institutions; wondering "who was that?"; making the memory significant or clue-like; adult interpretation.
- Memory is body-level, not thought-level. It arrives and fades.

## X. Physical Movement Map

## XI. Blocking Notes
- Room must be SMALL (emphasize containment; smell can't escape).
- Blanket must be STORED (not recently used; the unfolding releases trapped smell).
- Window: present but not open wide (smell stays).
- Absolute solitude throughout. No interruptions.
- Memory delivery: max 300 words in prose. Keep it brief, sensory, incomplete.
- Do NOT show the quiet man's face or body in memory (sound + smell + absence).
- Chappals sound = the only identifying detail allowed (and not identifying — everyone wears them).

## XII. Motif Callbacks

- Rubber chappals = anonymous presence (echoes in Ch12 when Shankarappa appears — writer-only connection; never narrate).
