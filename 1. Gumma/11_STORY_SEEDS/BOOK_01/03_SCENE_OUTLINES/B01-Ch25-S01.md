## 1. Scene Objective
Shankarappa is back. Meera discovers him on the courtyard veranda — tea, rubbing his bad knee, no announcement. She chooses to sit/wait nearby. After a long silence, he speaks the admission: "The terms I spoke — they are what I was told. I believe they are accurate. I cannot prove them beyond my telling." **2 RP (R6).** Judgment humility demonstrated. P-ABSENCE closes.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Rain-morning freshness → surprised relief (he's here) → waiting (disciplined; learned from Ch21/24) → receiving (the admission lands) → disappointment that transforms into respect (this is not a man with documents; this is a man carrying memory and saying he might be wrong).

## 3. Scene Conflict
Meera wanted certainty (about the terms, about him, about the system she's glimpsing). His return offers: honesty-with-limits instead. The conflict is between the desire for a savior (someone who KNOWS definitively) and the harder reality of a man who carries imperfect memory and says so. This disappoints and elevates simultaneously.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
She can't ask directly (she's learned: you wait with this man). She can't demand proof (he's just admitted there is none beyond his telling). She can't celebrate his return (he didn't leave for her; he didn't return for her). She must receive what's offered and hold the uncertainty. The obstacle is: living with doubt while trusting anyway.
