## 1. Scene Objective
Departure morning. Meera leaves the Hegde house. Bookend gestures land: Lakshmi's hand on cheek (Ch6 mirror); folded blanket visible through guest room door (Ch6 smell-memory); sandals spot bare (Ch12 absent — work-complete). Ramesh: awkward nod → **"Come sooner next time."** (DEC-A/ISS-04 payoff). Saroja: logistics-as-love. The house holds her differently than when she arrived. Leaving costs because she belongs.

## 2. Scene Emotional Objective
Pre-departure sadness (bag packed; short night after Ch29) → bookend warmth (gestures echoing arrival; the loop closing) → Ramesh's warmth landing (earned from Ch25 silence — the nod; the words) → grief sharpening (she's actually leaving; carrying more than she arrived with; this costs).

## 3. Scene Conflict
Departure vs belonging. She must leave (life in BLR; work; identity there). But she belongs HERE now — earned through weeks of watching, waiting, witnessing. The conflict is: having to leave a place that now holds you, when you arrived not knowing if it would.

## 4. Scene Obstacle
She can't stay (she has a city life). She can't take the village with her (it stays; she goes). She can't articulate what she learned (Lakshmi's sentence is internal; the path-peace is witnessed not owned). She can only: leave with dignity, carry what she has, and accept the grief of motion.

- Ramesh giving a speech (ONE LINE; one nod; then: gone; the brevity is earned from Ch25).

- The house-departure becoming a tour (she passes things; doesn't linger; the bookends are brief visual hits, not narrated museum-walk).
- Over-warming (the grief should be CLEAN — not weepy; not melodramatic; the dignity of leaving-with-love, not the collapse of separation).
- Making the scene too long (1,600 words for departure morning is enough; don't extend into excessive domestic).
