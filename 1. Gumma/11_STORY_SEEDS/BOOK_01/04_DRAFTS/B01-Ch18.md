# Chapter 18 — Unsent Email

## S01 — Three Drafts

Saturday evening in Bengaluru had its own weather — warm air through the window, traffic thinning to a hum, apartment lights multiplying in the buildings across the lane like a second city built from electricity and the loneliness of people who had not planned to spend another weekend alone.

Nav's flat was quiet in the way a place was quiet when it belonged to only one person.

Meera had never lived here. Her books were not on his shelf. Her slippers were not by the door. The couch knew only his weight, the impression of a man who came home late and left early and had learned to cook badly out of necessity rather than interest. A plant on the windowsill had survived through neglect and occasional guilt — green despite him, which felt like an accusation and a kindness at once. City sounds came and went: a horn, a motorcycle fading, someone's television through thin walls. Inside: the glow of a laptop screen and a blank email draft waiting to become something irreversible.

He had been stewing since the corridor. Since Vikram's smile and the postponed clarity meeting and the second postponement after that, each one delivered with the warmth of a man who believed warmth was a form of payment. Since the Series A happened and the equity stayed verbal and the car conversation in 2021 — *we'll take care of you when this closes, Nav, you know that* — became a memory Vikram treated like weather. Present once. Not actionable now. Four years. A team he had built from three people to nineteen. Market salaries forty percent higher than what loyalty paid. Interviews he had taken quietly, ashamed and practical at once, the shame not about leaving but about having stayed so long on a promise that lived in a parking lot and nowhere else.

Tonight he would write the letter. Make it real.

The flat around him held the evidence of a life assembled in increments — bookshelf with technical manuals and one novel he had finished and three he had not, a mug with a chipped handle, takeout menus in a drawer he never used because Meera said delivery made you soft. Saturday evening light through the window turning orange then grey. The laptop warm on his thighs. A blank draft waiting like an empty stage.

He typed.

*After careful consideration, I have decided to pursue other opportunities that align with my career goals and professional development...*

He read it. The words sat on the screen like furniture in a showroom — correct, lifeless, designed for someone else's approval. Deleted it. The professional version was surrender dressed as courtesy. Leaving politely so Vikram could keep the theft and the warmth in the same pocket and tell the board that Nav had moved on for personal reasons.

He typed again.

*You promised me equity in a car in 2021 and I believed you and that makes me an idiot.*

Read. Deleted. The honest version was worse — rage that sounded like weakness, naivety confessed in a font Vikram would forward to someone who handled exits, someone who would use the word *emotional* in a way that made Nav the problem for having believed a promise everyone in startup culture pretended was sacred until it wasn't.

An hour of fragments. Bits of both. Neither landing. Sentences that started true and ended performative. Sentences that started performative and curdled before the period. He tried a version that listed accomplishments — releases shipped, hires made, nights spent when Vikram called from investor dinners saying *we're almost there* — and deleted it because accomplishments were exactly what Vikram would use to argue he had already been paid in experience.

He tried a version that named numbers — the gap between his salary and what recruiters were offering — and deleted it because numbers made him sound mercenary and the betrayal was not mercenary, it was personal, it was *you said* and *I stayed* and *you did not do what you said*.

The cursor blinking with the patience of something that did not care how long he took or how many versions he murdered on a Saturday night.

He stopped. Stared at the kitchen doorway.

Memory arrived without permission — Meera in that kitchen years ago, visiting, not staying, laughing at his dal. Burnt cumin rising from the pan like a small domestic disaster. Smoke that made them both cough. Her face not unkind — amused in the way of someone who had already decided his failures were not disqualifying. *You're hopeless but you tried.* The particular relief of being known in failure. Not fixed. Seen. The smell of burnt spice still vivid enough that for a second he looked toward the stove as if the memory had left residue.

The warmth made the loneliness sharper.

She was in the village. Two days ago her text had appeared on his screen: *Things wild here. Tell you later.* He had not pushed. She had not explained. Tonight they were both in storms the other could not enter — his with a laptop and a blank *To:* field, hers with whatever *wild* meant when you were back in a house that had become a country with its own laws.

He glanced at his phone. The thread sat there. He did not reply.

Returned to the screen.

Something in the memory's honesty had infected his fingers. He started again — not professional, not begging, not cruel.

*I was told something in 2021 that shaped my next four years. It wasn't honored. I don't need you to explain why. I need to leave before the person I blame becomes me.*

He read it.

It said what he meant. It did not ask for remedy. It did not perform hurt for an audience. It was the letter of a man who had already left inside himself and was only now trying to make the outside match — direct, not theatrical, naming the betrayal without offering Vikram a script for apology that would let them both pretend the years were a misunderstanding.

Perfect.

His finger hovered over the trackpad. Send lived somewhere below the draft — one gesture from real. One click and Monday became a different country. Bridges burned or bridges crossed, but at least movement. At least the end of stewing.

The *To:* field was empty.

He had not typed Vikram's email. Had not even clicked into the line. The letter existed without a recipient. Complete and going nowhere. The gap between having decided and having acted: enormous, quiet, familiar — the width of one address he had not yet written, one line of text that would make the draft enter the world and become evidence instead of confession.

The cursor blinked in the blank line where a name should go.

Below it, the letter waited — every word earned through an evening of deleting the wrong versions of himself.

He read the final version once more. *Before the person I blame becomes me.* That was the truest line — not because Vikram deserved less anger, but because anger kept him here in a different way, kept him attached to a man who had already taken four years and might take more if Nav let the taking become identity.

His finger hovered again. Send. Delete. Send. The trackpad smooth under his thumb. The *To:* field still blank — Vikram's address memorized since the first week of employment and still not typed, as if typing it would make the betrayal mutual.

Still the *To:* field was empty.

The cursor blinked in the blank line where a name should go. The letter perfect below. The send not done.

## S02 — Dark Flat

He closed the laptop.

The screen went dark. The letter sealed inside — words that named the betrayal, unsent, unreal to anyone but him. He sat with the closed lid for a moment, as if the machine might speak through plastic. It did not.

He stood. The flat felt smaller with the glow gone — books and plant and couch becoming shapes in the dimness from the window.

In the kitchen he filled the kettle. Tea bag in cup. The routine of hands that were not deciding anything. Water heating. Steam later, when it came. His body on autopilot while his mind stood at the threshold it had refused to cross — the narrow space between *I know* and *I did*.

He checked his phone.

Nothing from Vikram. Saturday night — of course. Vikram was somewhere with people who laughed at the right moments, not thinking about Nav, not thinking about equity, not thinking about a man in a flat drafting his own exit while the city did its Saturday. The silence was confirmation of everything the letter said without needing to say it again.

Meera's thread sat below. *Things wild here. Tell you later.* Two days old. He read it again and did not reply. Did not push. She was in her own storm. He was in his. Neither could reach the other tonight — not neglect, just geography and timing and the mutual discipline of two people who had learned not to demand explanations when *later* was offered honestly.

The kettle clicked off.

He poured. Drank standing, cup warm against his palms, eyes on the closed laptop across the room. Silver edge catching kitchen light. A small object holding a large decision he had not yet made executable.

*If I send this, Monday is different.*

*If I don't, I'm the same person Tuesday morning.*

Worse: *I'm the person who drafted and didn't send.*

That identity sat in his chest — uncomfortable, honest. Not cowardice exactly. Not clarity either. The state of a man who had found the words and not yet found the action. Who had done the harder part — naming — and stopped before the part that required someone else to know. Tonight he was still that man. Because he was not sending tonight. Because the letter could wait until morning and the morning could wait until he knew what morning cost and whether cost was the same as price.

He washed the cup. Set it in the sink with the quiet clink of ceramic on steel. The kitchen light made the closed laptop across the room look like a closed book — knowledge inside, cover shut, title unknown to anyone who did not open it.

Tomorrow he would wake and the draft would still exist. He could send it before standup. He could delete it and pretend tonight never happened. He could revise one more time until the words lost their nerve. All of those futures were real. None of them were tonight.

Bed. Lights off. Phone face-down on the bedside table so the screen could not tempt him with blue ticks or the illusion of company. The flat went dark — plant and books and couch becoming nothing. Through the window the city continued without reference to his crisis, which was both insulting and correct.

The laptop would be there tomorrow on the couch. The draft would be there. The cursor, when he opened it, would still blink in an empty *To:* field, patient, indifferent, waiting for an address that would make the letter real to someone besides him.

He lay in the dark and listened to the building settle — pipe, distant door, the ordinary sounds of a life continuing in adjacent boxes — and slept with the decision unmade and the letter living only in the one place that did not require anyone else's acknowledgment to exist.

Real in his experience. Invisible to the world. Exactly that wide.
