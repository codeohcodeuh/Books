## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
End Act I with physical violence. The path dispute leaves the realm of words and enters bodies. Prasad (Gowda-side youth) shoves a Hegde-side worker; fence post breaks; someone is bruised. This is the act-ending event that transforms the dispute from "family tension" to "potential police matter." The comfortable assumption ("this will pass") dies here.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Act I has no climax; the dispute never becomes physical; P-POLICE never opens; Ch.11 (police statements) has no cause; the novel's escalation curve lacks its first crisis point; reader's hope that this is just a "family drama" story is never shattered.

**Promise job:**  
P-POLICE opens — legal/police intervention now threatens. P-PATH advances from simmering to crisis. This is the moment the dispute becomes public record.

**Emotional job:**  
Dread. The cold certainty that something has crossed a line and cannot uncross. After the comic relief of Ch.9, this is the hammer.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. The violence happens BECAUSE there's no institutional memory to ground either side. If the 1962 terms were accessible, this shove might never have happened. The absence is the cause. But text doesn't say this — only structure shows it.

**Unresolved pressure entering:**  
All of Act I tensions converging. Wedding in 10 days. Both sides posturing on the path. Youth carrying elders' honor as assignment.

**Reader assumptions currently true:**  
Dispute is serious but verbal; families still functioning around it; wedding might still smooth things.

**Reader assumptions we will not correct this chapter:**  
None. This chapter corrects the reader's last comfortable assumption: that violence won't happen.

## 10. Drafting Constraints

**Prose must avoid:**  
Action-movie choreography; narrator moralizing about violence; making Prasad evil (he's a scared young man who pushed too hard); making the worker's injury either trivial or melodramatic.

**Forbidden exposition:**  
Rural violence statistics; caste dynamics; police system explanation; path history; legal options.

**Forbidden lore:**  
All institutional content. No "if only there were someone who remembered."

**Forbidden vocabulary (Manual Appendix B + chapter-specific):**  
ancient order · chosen · destiny · for centuries · secret society · Holder · Verifier · Connector · Walker · Transmitter · Covenant · Article · Gumma · civilizational memory · mystical · supernatural · steward · custodian · rememberer · invisible · mediator · peacekeeper · could have prevented · 1962

**Invisible layer limits:**  
Zero. The absence is structural, never stated.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Ramesh. Prasad perceived externally (terror on his face — Ramesh sees it but doesn't sympathize with it in the moment). Worker perceived with protective attention. Meera barely present (glimpse at end; she sees worker but not the story).

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
Minimal. Path-confrontation: "This is our side" / "All sides are for walking" — brief, hot. Worker after: "Anna, leave it." Saroja: one plea-sentence. No speeches. Mostly bodies and silence.

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
Whether Ramesh will go to police; whether Prasad was ordered or self-motivated; whether the worker's "leave it" is kindness or fear; whether wedding can survive this.
