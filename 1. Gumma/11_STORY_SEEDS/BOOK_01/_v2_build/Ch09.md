## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Provide breathing room. After six chapters of escalating tension (wire, failed meeting, misdirection), the reader needs a comic relief beat. Introduce Farooq's voice (store owner, neutral, funny) and the temple committee as status-stage. Show Meera observing village politics with her dry urban eye — amused, detached, slightly superior. Plant Farooq's personality before he becomes important in Ch.24 and Ch.27.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Reader has no breathing space between Ch.8 unease and Ch.10 violence; Farooq has no introduction; temple-as-status-stage isn't established; Meera's observational voice in village context doesn't develop; emotional curve has no recovery beat between Act I dread-beats.

**Promise job:**  
None. This chapter services existing promises indirectly (P-WED continues ticking; P-PATH exists as background tension visible in how families interact at temple). No new promises opened.

**Emotional job:**  
Amusement. The specific warmth of watching adults fight over Rs. 5,000 donation differences and plastic chair arrangement at a temple function — petty, human, familiar.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. Purely civilian. The temple committee is secular politics in sacred clothing — no institutional layer. Farooq's presence (Muslim shopkeeper comfortable in temple logistics) establishes the village's actual communal texture.

**POV entering emotion:**  
Mild boredom; dragged along by Saroja ("come see the temple area for the wedding reception function").

Meera knows: there's a combined wedding reception function at the temple hall (both families); she's been asked to "help coordinate" (excuse to include her); she expects tedium.

Village is tense but functioning; wedding prep continues; life goes on around the dispute.

**Reader assumptions we will not correct this chapter:**  
That Farooq is just a shopkeeper (he is — but his node-function in the novel comes later).

**Prose must avoid:**  
Condescending to village governance; making temple politics a punch-down joke; Farooq as stock "wise minority character"; Meera's amusement becoming superiority the narrator endorses.

**Forbidden exposition:**  
Temple trust structures; donation economics; how village committees work; Farooq's personal history; communal relations lecture.

**Forbidden lore:**  
All institutional content.

**Forbidden vocabulary (Manual Appendix B + chapter-specific):**

**Invisible layer limits:**  
Zero.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Meera. Committee members perceived externally (dialogue + body language). Farooq: external (voice, grin, words) — warm but reader doesn't enter him. Saroja: external (business mode).

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
Committee members speak in meeting-registers (self-important; clause-citing; petty). Farooq speaks in shop-warmth (jokes, directness, casual Kannada-mixing if in English passages). Meera speaks minimally — she's observing, not participating. The humor is in the situation, not in Meera's witty remarks.

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
Whether Meera's warmth at end is genuine belonging-beginning or condescending amusement she'll later regret.
