## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Comic relief with thematic function. The apartment AGM (parking, CCTV, maintenance fund) is the urban micro-version of the village path dispute — same human pattern (contested shared space; whose rules?) in a completely different costume. For the first time, Meera sees the rhyme herself: "This is the same fight. Smaller scale. Same structure." P-APT closes as truce (not victory, not friendship — pragmatic coexistence with Asha). Same-day BLR round-trip fatigue grounds the comedy in real bodily cost.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
P-APT never closes; the urban-rural thematic rhyme never becomes conscious for Meera; the novel loses its comic-air beat at this position; reader has no breathing room between Ch18 loneliness and Ch20 new mystery; Nav warmth thread loses a natural ping window.

**Promise job:**  
P-APT closes as truce. Not resolution; coexistence. Asha and Meera will never be friends. But the parking fight is over because it's absurd — and Meera now SEES it's absurd because she's lived the village version.

**Emotional job:**  
Amusement — the healing kind. The click of recognizing a pattern across scales. Brief, warm, slightly melancholic (because seeing the pattern means she's changed).

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. Purely civilian thematic work.

## 3. Opening State

**POV entering emotion:**  
Bone-tired. She night-bused to Bengaluru Sunday night (arrived Mon dawn, slept three hours on the cousin's sofa). Worked a full Monday — client fire-drill, skipped lunch, back-to-back calls. Now AGM at 6 p.m. Her spine hurts from the bus seat. She wanted to skip but proxy wasn't arranged in time. Present grudgingly, running on coffee and spite.

**Other characters — entering emotions:**

**Unresolved pressure entering:**

**Reader assumptions currently true:**  
Apartment fight is petty and unresolved; Meera is city-competent; village arc is separate and heavier.

**Reader assumptions we will not correct this chapter:**
