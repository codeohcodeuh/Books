## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Show Meera's first attempt at mediation — and its failure. She tries to use her intelligence and urban-clarity to broker peace between cousins/young people from both sides. It doesn't work. Not because she's wrong, but because she hasn't earned standing here. Intelligence ≠ legitimacy. This is the humbling that sets up her later acceptance of Shankarappa's different authority (one earned through presence, not brilliance). Ananya witnesses and offers the chapter's micro-recovery: kind truth without false comfort.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Meera never tries and fails at mediation; the legitimacy-contrast with Shankarappa never establishes; Ch.16 arrives without reader knowing that civilian intelligence can't solve this; Meera's humbling arc doesn't begin.

**Promise job:**  
P-PATH advances through failure — the dispute is proven unsolvable by ordinary civilian intervention.

**Emotional job:**  
Regret. Specifically: the regret of overstepping. Of having good intentions and insufficient standing. Of being right about the problem but wrong about her right to fix it.

**Agency note (ISS-02):**  
Meera's failed mediation is itself her choice to act — already present; Ananya's POV frames the attempt and its cost.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. Meera's failure IS the argument for institutional function — but it's never stated. The reader simply sees: a smart person tried to help and it didn't work. Why? Because helping here requires something she doesn't have (presence over time, earned trust, acknowledged authority). Those qualities are what the institution provides — but the novel doesn't say that yet.

**Incorrect interpretations encouraged:**  
- Maybe a legal solution? Maybe time? Maybe elders just need to talk?

**Correct interpretation (writer only — not for prose):**  
- What's needed is someone with long presence + acknowledged authority + no stake in the outcome. That describes Shankarappa's function exactly. His legitimacy comes from decades of being present without owning land, having interests, or making demands. Meera's attempt fails not because mediation is impossible — but because mediation requires standing that only comes from sacrifice (presence over time; no extraction). The institution's power is: it has people who've paid that cost. Civilian Meera hasn't. Story doesn't say this yet.
