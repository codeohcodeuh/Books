## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
The novel's final beat. Lean-forward ambiguity. Meera witnesses a micro-quarrel in a city street — and a stranger's timing intervention (not dramatic; a single well-placed presence or word that de-escalates before Meera can fully register what happened). She can't be sure she saw what she saw. The stranger is already walking away. The novel ends on: "Was that—?" without answer. The reader is left with the QUESTION, not the answer. This is the series lean-forward: the world might be larger than one village. Or Meera might be projecting. Both are valid. Neither is confirmed.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
The novel ends purely in village resolution (too closed); the series-hook never plants; the urban extension of the theme never suggests; the reader's final emotion is completion rather than alive-question; P-CODA never opens.

**Promise job:**  
P-CODA opens — deferred to B2/series. The final promise: the world is larger than this book showed you. Maybe.

**Emotional job:**  
Unease + hope. The specific cocktail of: "I might be seeing things. But if I'm not — then the world is held by more than I know." That IS hope in the canon definition.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
**1 RP (R8). Final spend. Cumulative = 14 (matches plan exactly).** On-page: city stranger's timing changes a micro-conflict. Reader interprets: "Meera projecting / pattern is real." Incorrect: confirmed urban cell. Correct (writer only): AMBIGUITY REQUIRED. The novel does NOT confirm whether this is institutional. It might be coincidence. It might be a Walker or Connector in a city context. The novel ends on the question, not the answer. The ambiguity IS the final beat. If the novel answers this, it betrays its own principle.

## 10. Drafting Constraints

**Prose must avoid:**  
Confirmation in ANY direction; the stranger being memorable/identifiable; Meera following; narrator providing closure; theatrical ending; "and she knew" or "and she realized" — she SUSPECTS, she QUESTIONS, she HOLDS.

**Forbidden lore / vocabulary:**  
All Appendix B. Absolutely zero institutional vocabulary. The Coda must read as: a woman on a commute who notices something that might be nothing. That's ALL.

**Invisible layer limits:**  
**1 RP.** The ambiguity IS the limit. If the prose leans even slightly toward confirmation, it fails. If it leans toward denial, it fails. The exact balance point: "I think I just saw—" and then: nothing. No confirmation. No denial. The stranger is already walking on.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Meera. Stranger: entirely external (silhouette, umbrella, direction, speed). Arguing men: external (voices, anger, then confusion). The inner experience is Meera's breath, Meera's pattern-recognition firing, Meera's inability to confirm.

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
ZERO from Meera. The stranger's one word/phrase: INAUDIBLE to Meera (rain covers it). Arguing men: brief raised voices (before) → confused silence (after). The Coda is almost entirely interior and sensory.

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
EVERYTHING. Was it institutional? Was it coincidence? Does it matter? Is Meera changed by the question itself? Yes — and that IS the ending.
