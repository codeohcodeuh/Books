## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Introduce Meera in her ordinary Bengaluru life through a minor humiliation that establishes her character (competent, dry, slightly isolated) and plants the novel's thematic seed: rules that are enforced without being fair feel different from agreements that are remembered without being enforced. The apartment parking dispute is the urban micro-version of the village path dispute — same human structure, different costume.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Reader has no grounding in Meera's city life; the urban-rural thematic rhyme (P-APT ↔ P-PATH) loses its first term; Chapter 19 AGM payoff has no setup; Meera's personality (wry distance, competence, pride) arrives unnamed.

**Promise job:**  
Opens P-APT (parking / governance war with Asha). Establishes that Meera lives in a world of petty contested agreements — none of which have quiet custodians. This absence is only visible in retrospect.

**Emotional job:**  
Reader experiences embarrassment with Meera. Small-stakes, recognizable, warm enough to like her. Not trauma; not heroism; ordinary adult indignity at being publicly corrected in a WhatsApp group. Per Blueprint Part IX reader recovery ("Laugh"): the *situation* may land as comic-recognizable for the reader even while Meera experiences it as genuine embarrassment — without narrator mockery.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. Purely civilian. No invisible layer. No hints. No mystery. This is the world without custodians — only rules, screenshots, and read receipts.

## 10. Drafting Constraints

**Prose must avoid:**  
Any sense that this apartment dispute is "lesser" compared to a coming village story; narrator condescension toward urban pettiness; interiority longer than ~80 words without interruption; narrator punching down for laughs.

**Forbidden exposition:**  
How apartment associations work in India; Meera's backstory with village/family (that belongs to Ch.3+); her job in detail (Ch.2).

**Forbidden lore:**  
All institutional content.

**Forbidden vocabulary (Manual Appendix B + chapter-specific):**  
ancient order · chosen · destiny · for centuries · secret society · the Order · Holder · Verifier · Connector · Walker · Transmitter · Covenant · Article · Gumma · civilizational memory · mystical · supernatural · initiation · the mission · invisible · custodian · rememberer · steward (in institutional sense)

**Invisible layer limits:**  
Zero. No hints. No foreshadowing. Purely civilian.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Meera only; no access to Asha's interior; uncle perceived externally.

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
No one discusses tradition, mediation, memory, or oral agreements philosophically. Dialogue is logistics, embarrassment, and rules-citing. Asha speaks in committee language (clause numbers, timestamps). Meera speaks minimally — she is outmaneuvered in words.

**Tone constraint (ISS-27):**  
Prose may allow the reader to find the situation comic-recognizable (apartment WhatsApp governance; public minor humiliation) while Meera's embarrassment remains sincere. Humor = recognition. Never narrator mockery of Meera, Asha, or "Indian apartment drama."

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
Whether Meera's silence at the end is dignity or avoidance — reader may read either.
