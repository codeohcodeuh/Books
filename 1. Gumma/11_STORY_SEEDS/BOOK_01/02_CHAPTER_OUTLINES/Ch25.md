## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Shankarappa returns. He restates the terms — AND admits uncertainty: "This is what I was told. I believe it is accurate. I cannot prove it beyond my telling." This admission (judgment humility) is MORE powerful than his Ch16 confidence — because it reveals: he CHOSE to carry imperfect memory as responsibility, not as weapon. He refuses the hero role. Meera witnesses someone exercise authority while admitting limits. This is the most profound institutional demonstration in the novel — power that includes its own doubt.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Shankarappa never returns; P-ABSENCE unresolved; the distinction between confidence and humility-in-function never demonstrates; the Ch16 reveal is the ONLY institutional moment (too thin for novel arc); reader never sees power that admits doubt.

**Promise job:**  
P-ABSENCE closed (he's back). P-GUEST advanced to deepest point (he admits limits — this is more mysterious/admirable than confidence would be).

**Emotional job:**  
Disappointment — but the kind that transforms into respect. He refuses to be the answer. He refuses to be certain beyond what memory can hold. This disappoints anyone wanting a savior — and elevates anyone wanting truth.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
**2 RP (R6).** On-page: returns + admits uncertainty while restating. Reader interprets: "He's careful." Incorrect: weak/unsure/fake. Correct (writer only): judgment humility — the hardest institutional virtue. A Holder must carry AND doubt simultaneously.
