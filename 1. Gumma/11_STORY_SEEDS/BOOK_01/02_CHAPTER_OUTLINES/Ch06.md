## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Meera arrives at the village — the threshold crossing. Establish re-entry shame (the guest who doesn't belong enough). In the sleep room, triggered by smell and texture, deliver the novel's first RP beat: a childhood sensory memory of a quiet man who ended a shout. Not explained. Not named. Pure echo. This plants P-CHILD and begins the thematic resonance that pays off when Shankarappa appears in Ch.12.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Meera's arrival has no scene; the childhood echo (R1 in Reveal Map) never plants; the sleep room's emotional architecture is lost; Ch.29 (Lakshmi's stopped sentence) has no sensory precursor; the novel's first institutional whisper never arrives.

**Promise job:**  
P-CHILD opens — a sense-memory that will resonate without resolving in Book 1. P-WED advances (Meera now physically in wedding territory).

**Emotional job:**  
Belonging — the specific fragile kind: wanting to fit into a house that knows you but that you've abandoned through absence. The childhood flash adds a ghost-layer: she belonged here once more deeply than she remembers.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
**1 RP.** R1 from Reveal Map. On-page: child Meera (~10, 2003) remembers a quiet man whose presence ended a shouting match between elders. Sensory only — no name, no explanation, no institutional framing. Reader reads it as "village had a wise elder." Correct (writer only): young Meera witnessed Holder work in its mildest civilian form.

## 10. Drafting Constraints

**Prose must avoid:**  
Nostalgia as sentiment; the memory becoming a set-piece flashback with dialogue; narrator explaining what the quiet man "really was"; Meera analyzing the memory intellectually; any language that makes the memory seem significant to adult Meera (she registers it and sleeps — it's not a clue to her).

**Forbidden exposition:**  
House architecture as tour; family tree explanations; village geography lesson; who the 2003 man was; institutional history.

**Forbidden lore:**  
All institutional content. Function names. Organization name. Any hint of network or structure.

**Forbidden vocabulary (Manual Appendix B + chapter-specific):**  
ancient order · chosen · destiny · for centuries · secret society · Holder · Verifier · Connector · Walker · Transmitter · Covenant · Article · Gumma · civilizational memory · mystical · supernatural · steward · custodian · rememberer · invisible · mediator · protector · guardian · peacekeeper · authority (institutional sense) · the man who · always there when

**Invisible layer limits:**  
**1 RP, maximum 300 prose words.** Sensory only: smell → memory → sound → quiet → footsteps → sleep. No names, no faces, no dialogue, no context beyond "elders were shouting; then someone was there; then it was quiet." Delivered as body-memory, not thought.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Meera. In the memory: child-Meera's senses (hearing, smell) — NOT adult interpretation. The memory is incomplete because a 10-year-old doesn't understand what she witnessed.

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
Lakshmi: three words maximum ("sleep now, kanamma" or equivalent). Saroja: logistics sentences (food? room? tomorrow). No one discusses the quiet man. No one discusses the path. No substantive information exchanged.

**DEC-G fail-if (binding):**  
Never confirm on-page in Book 1 that the childhood quiet man is Shankarappa or the same person as the Ch12+ guest. Resonance allowed; identity closure forbidden.

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
Whether the memory is real or dream-inflected; whether the quiet man was family or outsider; whether Meera will think about this again (she won't, consciously, for many chapters).
