## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
Escalate the path dispute from internal (Ramesh's silent discovery) to interpersonal (fathers via sons/proxies arguing in pseudo-polite registers). Show that the alliance is cracking in public — the wedding-week politeness cannot hold. The teacups untouched = hospitality refused without saying "no."

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Path dispute never goes public; Ch.10 shove arrives without visible escalation curve; Ramesh–Venki relationship has no face-to-face deterioration scene; reader doesn't see how Indian family diplomacy fails in real-time.

**Promise job:**  
P-PATH advances — dispute now visible to both families. No new promise; existing pressure increases.

**Emotional job:**  
Dread. The reader should feel the specific cold of a politeness system breaking down — tea offered, tea not touched, the insult registered by every body in the room.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
0 RP. Purely civilian. The absence of any arbiter or memory-holder is felt structurally — no one in this scene can say "here is what was agreed." Both sides assert. Neither can prove. The dispute is intractable precisely because no custodian is present. Writer knows; text does not editorialize.

## 3. Opening State

**POV entering emotion:**  
Stiff formality. Ramesh agreed to this meeting (requested by Saroja via Venki's wife) to "clear the air before the wedding" — but he has no intention of backing down.

**POV entering knowledge:**  
Ramesh knows: wire was moved (Ch4); he hasn't confronted directly; family pressure says he must at least meet; the wedding alliance requires basic coordination (shared costs, hall, priest timing). He expects polite nothing.

**Other characters — entering emotions:**
- Venki Gowda: also stiff; also forced by women-folk to attend; considers himself reasonable; believes path discussion is "old topic settled years ago"
- Arjun (Ramesh's son, phone call earlier this morning): advised Ramesh "don't bring it up; handle after wedding"
- Saroja (offstage, organized this): nervous; hoping it goes well

**Unresolved pressure entering:**  
Wire (Ch4); Ananya's anger (Ch5, swallowed); Meera just arrived (Ch6, asleep recovering from bus). Wedding in 13 days.

**Reader assumptions currently true:**  
Dispute is real but manageable; families can still talk; wedding will probably happen fine.

**Reader assumptions we will not correct this chapter:**  
That tea-politeness means actual goodwill.

## 10. Drafting Constraints

**Prose must avoid:**  
Making either man clearly right; explaining Karnataka social customs to reader; tea-ceremony-as-metaphor explicitly stated by narrator; Meera becoming investigator here.

**Forbidden exposition:**  
Path history; 1962 terms; land-ownership law; family tree recap; wedding budget details.

**Forbidden lore:**  
All institutional content. No "rememberer" concept. No "mediator" invoked. No one suggests finding someone who "knows."

**Forbidden vocabulary (Manual Appendix B + chapter-specific):**  
ancient order · chosen · destiny · for centuries · secret society · Holder · Verifier · Connector · Walker · Transmitter · Covenant · Article · Gumma · civilizational memory · mystical · supernatural · steward · custodian · rememberer · invisible · mediator · someone who knows · the old agreement · terms (formal) · 1962

**Invisible layer limits:**  
Zero. Absence is structural, not stated.

**POV restrictions:**  
Close-third Ramesh. Venki perceived externally — reader cannot know his actual intentions. Meera perceived in passing (brief exterior moment from Ramesh's eyes). Saroja perceived in one sentence + body language.

**Dialogue restrictions:**  
Both men speak in controlled, indirect register. No shouting. No threats. The aggression is in sentence structure and what's unsaid. "All fathers walked it" is the sharpest line. Saroja gets one question ("How did it—") cut off. Meera: zero dialogue (nod only).

**Allowed ambiguity:**  
Whether Venki is aggressive or merely practical; whether Ramesh's silence about the wire is strategic or cowardly; whether tea-untouched was mutual or sequential.
