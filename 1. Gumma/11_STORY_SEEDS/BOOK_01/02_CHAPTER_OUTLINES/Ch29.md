## 2. Chapter Purpose

**Why this chapter exists:**  
The novel's deepest emotional reveal. Late night, intimate, Meera asks Lakshmi: "Ajji, who is he? Shankarappa-avare. What kind of person is he?" Lakshmi gives ONE framing sentence ("He keeps things from becoming police") and then STOPS. The sentence is mid-thought. She stops not because she can't continue but because she WON'T. This is love-as-withholding: protecting Meera from knowledge that would burden her, or protecting the institution from exposure, or both. The unfinished sentence IS Book 1's final institutional beat — it deepens the mystery while offering one warm, human frame.

**Removal test (what breaks if deleted):**  
Lakshmi's arc never culminates; the reader never gets the one-sentence framing that makes the novel's invisible layer emotionally comprehensible; P-LAK never opens properly; the Meera–Lakshmi intimacy (the novel's warmest relationship) has no peak; the mystery never reaches its deepest human form.

**Promise job:**  
P-GUEST reaches its deepest point (one sentence of framing + deliberate stop). P-LAK deepened (she KNOWS and WON'T say — B2/B3 must address).

**Emotional job:**  
Loneliness — the specific kind of being loved by someone who won't give you what you need. Not cruelty. Love expressed AS limitation. The stopped sentence hurts because it comes from warmth, not coldness.

**Institutional / RP job:**  
**2 RP (R7).** On-page: Lakshmi's anti-escalation framing + stop. Reader interprets: "Family knows a type of person." Incorrect: She'll finish later this book. Correct (writer only): Adjacent awareness; sacrifice of explanation. Lakshmi is pattern-aware (she likely refused induction in her youth or was adjacent through marriage). Her withholding is protective of both Meera and the institution.
