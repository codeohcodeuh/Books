# Gumma Universe — Novel Writing Production Manual
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Required before drafting prose  
**Last Reviewed:** 2026-08-02  
**Authority:** Production rules only. Does **not** override Canon, Blueprint, or Series Bible.  
**Scope:** Binding for Book 1 and all future novels unless a later Locked amendment says otherwise.

---

## Purpose

Create the operating system that prevents drift while drafting 80,000–100,000 words.

This is NOT the novel.  
This is NOT storytelling advice.  
This is NOT canon.

This is the production court: every future chapter must be validated against this manual before it is considered draft-complete.

**Hierarchy of authority (highest → lowest):**
1. Locked Canon (`00_CANON/`, Accepted/Locked `01_FOUNDATIONS/`)
2. Approved Blueprint for the active book
3. Series Bible (`11_STORY_SEEDS/00_SERIES_BIBLE.md`)
4. **This Production Manual**
5. Chapter/scene outlines
6. Draft prose

If prose conflicts with 1–3, prose loses. If prose conflicts with this manual, prose is revised or the manual is formally amended (never silently ignored).

---

## Assumptions

1. Different writers and AI models may draft different chapters; style must remain governed by rules, not vibes.
2. Mystery collapse is a production failure, not a creative flourish.
3. Lore hunger is the enemy of literary civilizational fiction.
4. A chapter can be beautifully written and still fail every quality gate.
5. Underspending the reveal budget is success. Overspending is a stop-work event.

---

# 1. NARRATIVE VOICE

---

## 1.1 POV Rules

- **Default:** Close third-person, one POV character per scene.
- **Chapter POV:** One primary POV per chapter. A brief second POV is allowed only if the Blueprint designates a dual structure for that chapter — never as lore convenience.
- **Allowed Book 1 POVs:** Only characters listed as POV in the Blueprint / Series Bible.
- **Gumma POV:** Forbidden in Book 1 except the Blueprint’s explicitly limited allowance (minimal, body-and-habit only, no doctrine). If used, maximum one short scene per act unless Blueprint amends.
- **Head-hopping:** Forbidden inside a scene.
- **Omniscient overview of the institution:** Forbidden forever unless a future Locked Blueprint authorizes a specific historical mode — and even then, not as secret-society tour guide.

---

## 1.2 Tense

- **Primary tense:** Simple past.
- **Present-tense gimmick chapters:** Forbidden.
- **Memory:** Past perfect sparingly; prefer timed flashback scenes framed as the POV’s present remembering, not grammar gymnastics.
- **Future speculation in narration:** Rare; belongs to character thought, not narrator prophecy.

---

## 1.3 Narrator Distance

- Stay **close** to the POV’s perceptual range: what they notice, misread, smell, resent, avoid.
- The narrator may clarify physical action the POV performs.
- The narrator may **not** clarify institutional meaning the POV does not have.
- Irony is allowed when it arises from the gap between what a character believes and what the reader suspects — without the narrator winking about “ancient custodians.”

**Distance test:** If a sentence could only be written by someone who has read the Foundations folder, cut it.

---

## 1.4 Interior Monologue

**Allowed when:**
- It reveals want, fear, embarrassment, calculation, or grief.
- It contradicts what the character says aloud (productive tension).
- It is short enough to feel like thought, not essay.

**Forbidden when:**
- It explains the Gumma, history, Covenant, Houses, or “how these people work.”
- It summarizes the novel’s theme in propositional language.
- It runs longer than ~90 words without interruption by action/dialogue/sensation.
- It diagnoses another character with clinical certainty the POV wouldn’t have.

---

## 1.5 Exposition

**Forbidden in narration:**
- Institutional primers
- Historical founding summaries
- Function/role definitions
- “For centuries…” claims
- Travelogue anthropology (“In Mandya district, it is customary…”)
- Thematic thesis statements

**Allowed:**
- Concrete facts the POV knows as life (who owns which field; when the bus leaves; that uncle is angry)
- Sensory world-building
- One-line orientation if spatially required (“She had not slept in that room since college”)

**Hard rule:** No paragraph may exist whose sole job is to teach the reader lore.

---

## 1.6 Sentence Rhythm

- Prefer clear, steady sentences. Vary length for breath, not for spectacle.
- Avoid stacked metaphors.
- Avoid thriller clipping (one-word paragraphs as default adrenaline).
- Allow quiet multi-clause sentences when a character is noticing carefully.
- Paragraphs: short when social pressure spikes; longer when attention rests on place or work.

**Prohibited cadence:** Constant cliffhanger staccato; sermon cadence; epic invocation cadence.

---

## 1.7 Dialogue Style

- Sounds like people mid-life, mid-chore, mid-argument.
- Interruptions, deflecting jokes, unanswered questions are normal.
- No character speaks Canon.
- No villain monologues; no mentor speeches about civilization.
- Code-switching is real: English in office, Kannada-dominant family space (rendered in English with occasional untranslated words where meaning is clear from context). Do not decorate speech with fake “broken English.”

---

## 1.8 Humor Rules

- Humor comes from situation, pride, logistics, WhatsApp governance, wedding absurdity — not from mocking villagers as backward.
- Characters may be ridiculous; communities may not be punchlines.
- Gumma-adjacent figures are not comic mystics.
- Cruel humor that punches down fails the Living World test.

---

## 1.9 Silence Rules

- Silence is action: refusal, protection, fatigue, strategy, love.
- Narrator must not translate every silence into meaning.
- After a charged silence, continue with physical business (tea, phone, fence wire) before interpretation.
- Characters may misunderstand silence. That misunderstanding is gold — do not correct it in narration.

---

## 1.10 Good Narration (Examples of Kind)

> Meera watched her mother rearrange the same steel boxes she had rearranged that morning, as if order could bully the wedding into behaving.

> Ramesh put his palm on the gatepost and felt the heat still in the wood. The wire had been moved again. Not far. Far enough.

> Shankarappa accepted a second cup he did not want and asked about the sugarcane, which made Venki talk long enough for the anger in the room to change shape.

---

## 1.11 Forbidden Narration (Fail Immediately)

> Little did she know that an ancient order had watched over her family for seven centuries.

> The Gumma had sent Shankarappa because Article IV required the living be prioritized.

> In the Deccan heartland, Holders still walked the old routes, invisible pillars of civilization.

> Something mystical stirred in the air between the two fields.

> He was not merely a man; he was Memory itself.

# 2. SCENE CONSTRUCTION FRAMEWORK

---

## 2.1 Mandatory Scene Questions

Every scene must answer, in the scene outline **before** drafting:

1. **Who wants what?** (concrete, local)
2. **What prevents it?** (person, pride, time, weather, money, fear, another want)
3. **What changes?** (information, relationship temperature, power balance, self-understanding — at least one)
4. **What emotional residue remains?** (what the character carries into the next scene)

If any answer is “to reveal lore,” the scene is rejected.

---

## 2.2 Scene Legality

A scene may include institutional texture **only if** it is the side effect of a human problem already in motion (dispute, wedding, police, illness, rumor).

A scene may not be scheduled because “we need to show a Connector.”

---

## 2.3 Repeatable Scene Template

Copy into every scene outline file:

```text
SCENE ID: B01-Ch__-S__
POV:
LOCATION:
TIME OF DAY / STORY DATE:
WEATHER:

WANT:
OBSTACLE:
TURN (what changes):
RESIDUE (emotion + factual state change):

ONSTAGE CHARACTERS:
OFFSTAGE PRESSURE (calls, rumors, clocks):

DIALOGUE PURPOSE (negotiate / deflect / wound / repair / logistics):
SILENCE PURPOSE (if any):

REVEAL POINTS SPENT THIS SCENE (0–n):
PROMISES CREATED / ADVANCED / CLOSED:
CONTINUITY FLAGS (injury, travel, phone, knowledge):

CANON RISK NOTES:
FAIL IF:
```

---

## 2.4 Scene Exit Rule

End on image, gesture, unanswered line, or practical next action — not on explanation.

# 3. CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE

---

## 3.1 Anatomy (Required Order of Functions)

Not every beat needs equal length, but all six functions must appear:

1. **Opening problem** — human, immediate, on the first page of the chapter. Never institutional orientation.
2. **Escalation** — the problem costs more than expected (time, face, money, love, safety).
3. **Human interaction** — at least one charged exchange (dialogue or deliberate silence between people).
4. **Quiet turn** — a shift in understanding, temperature, or allegiance that is not loud.
5. **Ending image** — concrete sensory close.
6. **Hook** — forward pressure (clock, dread, hope, unanswered question) without thriller bait-and-switch.

---

## 3.2 Targets (Book 1 defaults; amend per book if Locked)

| Metric | Target | Hard limits |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Chapter length | 2,200–3,200 words | Min 1,600 / Max 4,000 (except coda) |
| Scenes per chapter | 2–4 | Max 5 |
| Dominant emotion | Exactly 1 | Secondary emotions allowed as spice, not co-leads |
| Emotional beats | 3–5 micro-shifts | Not a mood buffet |
| Reveal points | 0–2 typical | Max 3 in a designated Reveal Chapter (Blueprint-marked) |
| Opening | Human problem by word 150 | Fail if lore-first |

**Novel length target:** 85,000–100,000 words.  
**Chapter count (Book 1):** ~30 + coda (per Blueprint).

---

## 3.3 Chapter Header Block (Required in draft file)

```text
CHAPTER:
POV:
STORY DATE:
DOMINANT EMOTION:
REVEAL POINTS USED:
SERIES BIBLE THREADS TOUCHED:
QUALITY GATE STATUS: Pass1 / Pass2 / Pass3 / Pass4 / Pass5
```

# 4. INFORMATION ECONOMY

---

## 4.1 Four Knowledge Layers

| Layer | Definition | May narration use it? |
|-------|------------|------------------------|
| **Reader-known** | Established on-page in prior approved chapters + this chapter’s shown events | Yes |
| **Character-known** | What this POV actually knows/believes | Yes, through that POV only |
| **Narrator-known** | Production/canon truth held by writer | **No** — narrator is not a lore channel |
| **Forbidden-to-explain** | Institutional doctrine, labels, centuries-claims, function maps | Never in Book 1 narration/dialogue as teaching |

---

## 4.2 Narrator Forbidden Explanations (Non-Exhaustive)

- What the Gumma are
- Why Shankarappa was “sent”
- How memory is trained
- House structure
- Covenant article names or paraphrased doctrine speeches
- That silence is “for the institution”
- That a character is a Holder/Verifier/Connector/Walker/Transmitter by label
- That an event is part of a seven-century pattern (as fact asserted by narrator)

---

## 4.3 Hard Anti-Exposition Rules

1. **No teacher scenes.** If a character starts explaining a system, interrupt with life.
2. **No glossary dialogue.** “You see, people like him…” fails.
3. **No reader-service summaries** at chapter ends (“So now Meera understood that…”).
4. **Misunderstanding stands.** If Meera thinks he’s a retired revenue officer, narration does not smirk a correction.
5. **Documents on-page:** Legal papers, chats, notes — civilian only. No secret ledgers.
6. **If you must convey history,** convey it as contested family argument, not archive voice.

---

## 4.4 Information Release Test

Before keeping a sentence, ask:

- Does a civilian POV have access to this?
- Does the scene’s want require it?
- Does withholding serve mystery **and** character truth?
- Would cutting it make the human story clearer?

If the sentence exists to reward the canon-reader, cut it.

# 5. REVEAL VALIDATION SYSTEM

---

## 5.1 What Counts as a Reveal Point

A **Reveal Point (RP)** is spent when on-page evidence meaningfully increases reader understanding of the invisible institutional layer.

**Examples that cost RP:**
- Specific unwritten past terms accurately recalled by a non-family outsider
- Clear refusal of payment/ownership that signals a code
- Evidence of more than one coordinated quiet actor
- A message ecology that implies network
- Explicit framing that such people exist to prevent escalation (e.g., Lakshmi’s line)

**Examples that cost 0 RP:**
- Family anger, wedding logistics, police procedure
- Civilian oral promises (Nav/ESOP) — thematic rhyme, not institutional reveal
- Atmosphere, rumor, wrong theories

---

## 5.2 Budgets (Book 1)

| Scope | Max RP | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Per ordinary chapter | **2** | Prefer 0–1 |
| Per Blueprint-designated Reveal Chapter | **3** | Ch. 16, 22, 25, 29 type only |
| Per Act | **Act I: 3 / Act II: 10 / Act III: 7** | Soft caps; do not front-load |
| Per novel (Book 1) | **18 RP total absolute ceiling** | Target ≤ 14; success if lower |
| Nuclear items | **0** | Word “Gumma”; Houses; Covenant; founding; training system; function labels |

If a draft would exceed a budget, **stop and cut** before proceeding to the next chapter.

---

## 5.3 Reveal Scoring Rubric

| Score | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 0 | No institutional layer advanced |
| 1 | Soft texture (respect without reason; timing luck that could be coincidence) |
| 2 | Clear anomalous competence/memory/refusal hard to dismiss |
| 3 | Pattern confirmation (network / code / multi-actor) — rare; Blueprint only |

A single speech cannot score 3 unless Blueprint pre-authorizes that beat.

---

## 5.4 Tracking Table (Copy into Series Bible after each chapter)

| Ch | Act | RP spent | Cumulative RP | What was revealed (one line) | Nuclear risk? Y/N | Bible updated? |
|----|-----|----------|---------------|------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| 1 | I | 0 | 0 | — | N | |
| 2 | I | 0 | 0 | — | N | |
| … | | | | | | |

---

## 5.5 Mystery Health Checks

After every chapter:

- Did questions increase at least as much as answers?
- Could a reader still summarize the book as a family/wedding/dispute novel?
- Did any line make the invisible layer feel like a known faction?

Any “No / faction-yes” = revision required.

# 6. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY RULES

---

## 6.1 Pre-Chapter POV Checklist

Complete for **every** POV appearing in the chapter before drafting:

| Check | Question | Pass? |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Speech | Does dialogue match education, region, stress, and relationship? | |
| Habits | Hands, tea, phone, posture, avoidance patterns intact? | |
| Humor | Right kind/amount for this character under this stress? | |
| Fears | Active fear influencing choices? | |
| Relationships | Current temperature with each onstage person accurate? | |
| Emotional state | Entering emotion matches prior chapter residue? | |
| Knowledge state | Knows only what Series Bible matrix allows? | |
| Recent events | Sleep, injury, travel fatigue, shame, triumph accounted for? | |
| Want | Chapter want is character-true, not plot-forced? | |
| Forbidden drift | Not smarter/more lore-aware than yesterday? | |

Fail any row → outline revision before prose.

---

## 6.2 Non-POV Onstage Characters

For each important onstage character, verify minimum:

- Last known emotional stake
- What they want in this scene
- What they must not suddenly confess

---

## 6.3 Gumma-Adjacent Consistency

- No sudden martial skill, hypnotic voice, perfect foresight.
- Competence is social, mnemonic, patient — and fallible.
- Fatigue, knee pain, irritation, ordinary errands required periodically.
- They may be wrong once per novel minimum (Book 1: at least one misread).

---

# 7. DIALOGUE RULES

---

## 7.1 Villagers / Agrarian Family Speech

- Practical, relational, status-aware.
- Arguments travel through third parties (“Tell your father…”) as often as direct address.
- Proverbs possible but not folkloric cosplay — max rare.
- Anger often coded as logistics (“Who will take the cart then?”).

---

## 7.2 Bengaluru Professionals

- Faster topic-switching; English/Kannada mix.
- Workplace conflict uses HR-safe language until private spaces unlock sharper speech.
- Irony and self-deprecation common; epic sincerity rare.

---

## 7.3 Older People

- More indirection; stories that seem to miss the point and don’t.
- Authority through pacing, not vocabulary.
- May stop mid-explanation (Lakshmi pattern) — do not complete it in narration.

---

## 7.4 Silence in Dialogue

- Use beats: pouring water, adjusting pallu/shirt, unlocking phone, looking at fields.
- “He didn’t answer” is stronger than a paragraph about why.

---

## 7.5 Disagreement

- People protect face while attacking substance.
- Public disagreement ≠ private disagreement.
- Escalation often happens after the polite sentence.

---

## 7.6 Avoiding Direct Answers

Allowed tactics: counter-question, anecdote, logistical pivot, joke, addressing a different person, leaving to take a call.

Forbidden tactic: mysterious oracle vagueness (“In time, you will understand.”)

---

## 7.7 Anti-Exposition Dialogue

Fail lines like:

- “As you know, our families agreed in 1962 that…”
- “People like Shankarappa exist to preserve civilizational memory.”
- “I’m not supposed to tell you this, but…”

Replace with disputed memory, partial recall, irritation, and practical stakes.

---

# 8. EMOTION FRAMEWORK

---

## 8.1 One Dominant Emotion Per Chapter

Before drafting, declare **exactly one** dominant emotional impression the chapter must leave.

**Approved palette (non-exclusive):**  
relief · regret · belonging · unease · embarrassment · hope · grief · anticipation · shame · tenderness · dread · loneliness · pride (costly) · amusement (warm) · disappointment

---

## 8.2 Guidance

- Dominant emotion is what a reader should name if asked immediately after reading.
- Other emotions may appear; they must serve or contrast the dominant, not compete equally.
- Do not chase “epic awe.” Awe is a genre drift alarm in this franchise.
- **Hope** means “tomorrow still worth serving,” not “everything will be fine” (`21`).
- **Unease** is the correct institutional emotion for Book 1 — not terror, not wonder.

---

## 8.3 Emotion / Plot Alignment Examples

| Chapter type | Likely dominant |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Wedding day | Belonging (with strain) |
| Path shove | Dread / embarrassment |
| Accurate remembering | Disappointment / unease |
| Bribe refusal / withdrawal | Unease |
| Settlement walk | Relief (incomplete) |
| Lakshmi stops speaking | Loneliness / unease |
| Coda | Unease + faint hope |

# 10. CANON VALIDATION

---

## 10.1 Hard Fail Checklist

A chapter **may not pass** if any item fails:

| # | Check | Fail condition |
|---|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Foundations / hard locks | Ghosts, ninjas, supernatural, prophecy, chosen one, gadgets, magic archives |
| 2 | Institution | On-page org chart, House system, training pipeline, named functions |
| 3 | Power | Domination fantasy; cool power display; institutional omnipotence |
| 4 | Trust | Instant trust without deposits; extraction framed as noble |
| 5 | Legitimacy | Announced authority; popularity = rightness |
| 6 | Judgment | Infallible rememberer; certainty theater without cost |
| 7 | Responsibility | Dumping blame as only engine; no forward carrying |
| 8 | Sacrifice | Martyr cosplay; suffering as proof of virtue; forced sacrifice as glamorous |
| 9 | Hope | Guaranteed outcomes; motivational speeches; cynicism as intelligence flex by narrator |
| 10 | Living World | Society treated as backdrop; Gumma as protagonists of the world |
| 11 | People / Lifecycle | Cartoon elders/youth; induction melodrama in Book 1 |
| 12 | Evolution | Instant ancient wisdom unchanged by modernity; phones ignored absurdly |
| 13 | Failures | Perfect custodians; no cost for absence/error |
| 14 | Invisible | Institution explained to reader |
| 15 | Reveal budget | Exceeds chapter/act/novel RP caps; nuclear reveal used |
| 16 | Series Bible | Contradicts registry, knowledge matrix, or open threads wrongly closed |
| 17 | Blueprint | Deviates from approved structure without formal amendment |
| 18 | Word “Gumma” | Appears on-page in Book 1 |

---

## 10.2 Soft Watch (Revise Strongly if Triggered)

- Village romantization
- Caste essentialism
- Copaganda or universal-corruption cartoon
- Journalist exposé genre takeover
- Meera-as-detective arc
- Shankarappa-as-Yoda speeches

# 11. QUALITY GATES — FIVE REVIEW PASSES

A chapter is **not done** until all five passes are checked. Order is mandatory.

---

## Pass 1 — Human Story

Questions:

1. Does the chapter begin with a human problem by word 150?
2. Can you state who wanted what and what changed without mentioning the institution?
3. Would this chapter still matter if all Gumma-adjacent characters were deleted (for non-Reveal chapters)?
4. Are wedding/work/family/police stakes concrete?
5. Is any scene present only to teach?
6. Does the ending image belong to life, not logo?

**Fail → rewrite story before polishing sentences.**

---

## Pass 2 — Character

Questions:

1. Pre-chapter consistency checklist completed and passed?
2. Does dialogue sound like distinct people?
3. Are mistakes character-true (pride, avoidance, humor)?
4. Did anyone become a mouthpiece?
5. Is Gumma-adjacent fallibility intact?
6. Do relationships move by inches, not speeches?

**Fail → character pass rewrite.**

---

## Pass 3 — Canon / Bible / Reveal

Questions:

1. Section 10 hard-fail checklist all pass?
2. RP counted and logged?
3. Knowledge matrix respected?
4. Promise ledger updated?
5. Continuity checklist passed?
6. Mystery health checks passed?

**Fail → stop-work until fixed. Do not proceed to language polish as consolation.**

---

## Pass 4 — Language

Questions:

1. Any forbidden narration patterns?
2. Exposition paragraphs?
3. Metaphor overload / thriller staccato / sermon tone?
4. Untranslated words clear in context?
5. Sensory specificity without cataloguing?
6. Could any sentence only be written by a Foundations reader?

**Fail → line edit.**

---

## Pass 5 — Emotional Impact

Questions:

1. Declared dominant emotion achieved?
2. Does the chapter leave residue into the next?
3. Is the feeling closer to quiet recognition than plot sugar-rush?
4. Did we earn silence?
5. Would a reader who hates “secret societies” still care about these people today?
6. Ishiguro/Robinson test (see §14): restraint, moral remainder, human scale — present?

**Fail → adjust turn/residue/ending image; do not add lore to “increase impact.”**

# 13. AI COLLABORATION RULES

---

## 13.1 Assume Model Rotation

Different AI models / human writers may draft different chapters. Continuity is enforced by artifacts (Bible, ledgers, this manual), not by model memory.

---

## 13.2 Required Context Pack (Every Drafting Session)

Before any model writes prose, the session must be given:

1. This Production Manual (or explicit acknowledgment of binding rules)
2. Relevant Blueprint sections (cast, chapter row, reveal map)
3. Series Bible excerpts: character registry rows, knowledge matrix, open threads, reveal ledger cumulative RP
4. Prior chapter ending + residue
5. Scene template answers for scenes being drafted
6. Ban list (§1.11, §10, Series Bible tone contract)

**If context is missing, do not draft.**

---

## 13.3 Anti-Drift Rules for AI

| Drift type | Prevention |
|------------|------------|
| Style drift | Enforce §1 rhythm; reject “luminous/ancient/whispered destiny” diction |
| Contradiction | Bible registry beats vibes; stop on conflict |
| Over-explaining | RP budget + exposition bans; prefer cutting |
| Mystery collapse | Questions ≥ answers check mandatory |
| Genre drift | Ban list; Pass 5 comparison qualities |
| Coolification | Remove competence spectacle; add fatigue/errands/error |
| Lore dumping | Scene legality test; no teacher scenes |
| Character IQ creep | Knowledge matrix freeze |

---

## 13.4 AI Output Validation

AI-produced chapters are **draft candidates only** until all five passes are human-confirmed (Creative Director or designated reviewer).

AI may propose RP scores; human locks the ledger.

---

## 13.5 Prompt Hygiene

Forbidden instructions to any model:

- “Explain the Gumma to the reader”
- “Make it more like a thriller”
- “Add a training flashback”
- “Have him reveal the order’s name”
- “Raise the stakes with a death threat from a secret society”

Required instructions:

- “Begin with human problem”
- “Spend at most N reveal points”
- “Dominant emotion = X”
- “Do not use the word Gumma”
- “Narrator may not know more than POV”

# 14. SUCCESS CRITERIA

---

## 14.1 Orientation (Not Imitation)

The finished novel should feel closer in **effect** to:

- Kazuo Ishiguro — restraint; moral remainder; what is not said
- Marilynne Robinson — ordinary holiness of attention; patience
- *A Man Called Ove* — human stubbornness and care without prestige plot
- *The Remains of the Day* — dignity, deferral, cost of vocation misread as mere personality

…rather than:

- *The Da Vinci Code* / Dan Brown — puzzle chase, clue-vomiting
- *Harry Potter* — initiation into a wondrous parallel world as primary pleasure
- Brandon Sanderson — system exposition as engine

**Do not imitate any author’s prose.** Measure qualities, not cadence cloning.

---

## 14.2 Measurable Qualities (Pass/Fail)

| Quality | Measurable test |
|---------|-----------------|
| Human primacy | Cold summary of plot needs no secret organization |
| Restraint | At least 3 major questions remain open at novel end |
| Moral remainder | Settlement leaves someone disappointed for good reason |
| Scale | Most scenes involve ≤ 6 speaking characters |
| Time | Attention given to chores, waiting, weather, food |
| Mystery texture | Invisible layer noticed as feeling before system |
| Anti-cool | Most competent quiet act looks dull in the moment |
| Language | <1 metaphor per page average in sample audit |
| Reveal discipline | Final RP total ≤ ceiling; nuclear = 0 |
| Emotional honesty | Dominant emotion logged per chapter; no “awe” default |
| Reread test | Second reading adds pattern recognition, not “I was spoon-fed” |
